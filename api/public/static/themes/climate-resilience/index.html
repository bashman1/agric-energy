Climate Resilience and Sustainability

How agri-energy investments contribute to climate adaptation and mitigation in Uganda's agricultural sector, building farmer resilience and reducing emissions.

About this Theme

Climate change is profoundly affecting Uganda's agriculture through erratic rainfall, prolonged droughts, flooding, shifting seasons, and increased pest and disease pressure. Agri-energy plays a dual role in climate resilience: first as an adaptation measure by enabling irrigation, improved food storage, and reduced climate risk; and second as a mitigation measure by replacing fossil fuels and reducing deforestation from firewood collection. This thematic area explores both dimensions, documenting how agri-energy interventions build farmer resilience to climate shocks, preserve ecosystems, reduce greenhouse gas emissions, and support Uganda's climate commitments under the Paris Agreement and the NDC framework.

Why It Matters

Agriculture in Uganda is predominantly rainfed and therefore highly vulnerable to climate variability. Agri-energy investments that enable irrigation, improved storage, and clean energy supply are also investments in climate adaptation. Without incorporating climate resilience into agri-energy programming, gains from technology adoption can quickly be reversed by climate shocks.
